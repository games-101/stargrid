Rules

  1. Place stars so that every row, every column and every outlined region contains exactly one star.
  2. Stars may not touch each other, including diagonally.
  3. Mark cells you have ruled out with a dot. Dots are notes only; the puzzle is solved when all stars are placed correctly.

Controls

  1. Tap a cell to cycle blank → dot → star. The Star and Dot modes make a tap toggle only that mark.
  2. Drag across blank cells to dot them in one stroke. Right-click toggles a dot.
  3. Keyboard: arrow keys move, S star, D or X dot, Space cycles, Backspace clears, Z/Y undo/redo.
  4. Cells are named by row and column, for example R3C5 is row 3, column 5. Regions are lettered A, B, C… from the top-left.

How difficulty is rated

Each puzzle is solved by a reasoning engine that always uses the simplest technique available. The rating depends on the hardest techniques it needed and how often it needed them.

Basic
A row, column or region has exactly as many open cells as stars still needed.
Simple
One-cell lookahead: a star here would leave a neighbouring unit without room. Also a region confined to a single row or column.
Intermediate
Counting across two units: two regions that fit inside two rows own all the stars of those rows.
Advanced
The same counting argument across three or more units at once.
Contradiction
Assume a cell and follow forced steps (basic and simple only) until a rule breaks.
Deep contradiction
A contradiction chain that itself needs counting arguments along the way.

Hard puzzles need counting across two or more units. Expert puzzles need several contradiction chains. Insane puzzles (11×11) need 15 or more contradiction chains, some of them 3 to 5 forced steps long.
